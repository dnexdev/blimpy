1. What you are building

A plain white latex balloon about 1.1 m across, with a small flat gondola clipped tight under it. On the gondola: the hardware team's breadboard (Bluetooth board, two motor drivers, the IMU), one battery, and four little motors: two at the back pointing forward (L and R), one sideways through the middle (S), one pointing down under the middle (V). Nothing else flies. The laptop watches the room with its webcam, talks to the gondola over Bluetooth, and does all the thinking. If the laptop goes quiet for half a second, every motor stops.

Numbers marked MEASURE are placeholders in laptop/config.py. Measure them on the bench, write them in, log them with the date in calib/MEASUREMENTS.md.

Why four motors, all reversible

A sphere has no keel. After a turn it keeps sliding the old way, and at these speeds drag will not stop it in time; only thrust does. So the two rear motors drive, brake, and steer; a sideways motor through the centre kills sideways drift; a vertical motor holds height, because lift changes by grams as the hall warms and helium leaks. Ballast alone puts it in the ceiling within a minute.

Trim the balloon about one gram heavy. On power loss it settles to the floor instead of the ceiling, and the vertical motor mostly pushes up, which is its efficient direction.

Why nothing else flies

There is no camera, microphone or speaker on the balloon. The laptop webcam is the room camera, the laptop mic and speakers (or AirPods) are the voice. Every gram saved on the gondola is a gram of lift margin, and the demo works in any room with the laptop, the mat board and the balloon.

2. The gondola, plan view

The breadboard is the gondola body. A stiff stick under its rear edge carries L and R. S sits on the right edge, V under the middle, the battery under the left half so the board hangs level. Forward is the direction L and R push.

  1. L and R point forward, parallel, 25 cm apart, at the rear. Both forward = drive. Both backward = brake. One stronger than the other = turn.
  2. S goes through the middle. Edge of the board is fine; in line with the middle of the board, not ahead of or behind it.
  3. V under the middle, prop clear. Nothing under it: no wires, no battery. (No ultrasonic on this box: it ran out of pins.)
  4. IMU flat, chip facing up, fixed to the board, not dangling on wires. Only the gyro's Z axis matters; one config flag fixes its sign after the bench check.
  5. Nothing within 3 cm of a prop disc: strings, wires, the balloon skin, fingers. Bridle strings meet the rear bar 3 cm inboard of the motors, in front of the prop plane.
  6. Every motor on soft foam tape, wires taped or zip-tied down. Vibration is the noise, and the IMU reads vibration as motion.
  7. Ultrasonic (if used) at the front edge, looking straight down, 10 cm or more from the V prop so the prop stays out of its 15° cone.
  8. Battery under the left half to balance S on the right; move it until the board hangs level from the ring.

6. Build order

Bench first, balloon last. Each step is a go / no-go for the next. Commands run from the repo root with Bluetooth on; README section 3 has the same bench list in more detail.

  1. The box talks before anything is built around it

    Power the breadboard, then on the laptop:

    python -m laptop.control.ble_gondola --probe

    It must connect to BalloonRobot and print an IMU line with a parsed dict (gz_rad present) 20 or more times a second. Turn the board counter-clockwise by hand: gz positive. If negative, set GYRO_SIGN = -1 in config.BLE.

  2. Motors spin, props off 
    python -m laptop.control.ble_gondola --motor C 30

    Two seconds of motor C at 30 %. Repeat for D, E, F and write down which physical motor each letter is. --motor C -30 must reverse it.

  3. Fill in the map

    In laptop/config.py, BLE["MOTORS"]: which letter is L (rear left), R (rear right), S (sideways), V (vertical). Label the motors with tape.

  4. Build the frame around the board

    Rear bar with L and R, S on the right edge, V under the middle on a standoff, IMU flat, battery under the left half, three bridle strings to a clip (sections 2 and 3). Props on now; balance each blade on a pin and sand the heavy tip until it stops settling to one side.

  5. Teleop on the bench, gondola tied down 
    python -m laptop.control.ble_gondola       # terminal 1: the bridge, leave it running
python -m laptop.control.teleop            # terminal 2: SPACE arms

    Hold the gondola still for 3 s, SPACE, then: w = L and R blow backward (gondola wants forward); s = both reverse; j = S blows right (gondola wants left); q = V blows down (gondola wants up); a = R harder than L and telemetry yaw rises. A motor pushing the wrong way: flip its SIGN to −1 in config.BLE.

  6. Failsafe

    Ctrl+C the teleop: everything stops within half a second. Then kill the bridge while the motors run: if they keep spinning, the firmware has no timeout yet (section 4). Until it has one, someone stays on the battery switch.

  7. Weigh it

    Kitchen scale, with battery and ballast. PHYS M_GONDOLA (kg) and the motor spacing into PHYS, both with the date into calib/MEASUREMENTS.md.

  8. Measure thrust

    One motor taped upright on the scale, prop blowing up and away from the pan. Teleop w ×3 / ×4 / ×5 (30 / 40 / 50 %), read grams: expect roughly 4.5 / 8 / 12 g for an 8520. T_MAX = 9.81e-3 × grams_at_50 / 0.25 newtons. Then s ×5 for reverse: REV_EFF = reverse / forward.

  9. Inflate

    To the size the weight allows: tape round the fattest part 3.46 m (1.10 m) or 3.83 m (1.22 m). Tie off, string loop and keyring round the knot, neck string short.

  10. Clip the gondola on

    Board level, every prop tip 5 cm or more from the skin. If not, lengthen the bridle strings a little.

  11. Trim

    Motors off. Coins or blu-tack at the gondola centre until, released at chest height, it takes 5-10 s to reach the floor. About one gram heavy. It will feel too heavy; it is not.

  12. Measure the hang

    Tape from the floor to the balloon's equator and to the motor plane. ARM_BELOW = equator minus motor plane; the inflated diameter into D (no ultrasonic on this box, so TOF_BELOW stays). All into PHYS and calib/MEASUREMENTS.md.

  13. Rerun the simulator with the real numbers 
    python tools/scenarios.py --seeds 3
python tools/control_test.py
  14. First flight: hover only 
    python -m laptop.vision.mono --auto-calib --show     # terminal 1: the room camera
python -m laptop.control.ble_gondola                 # terminal 2: the bridge
python -m laptop.control.pilot --no-voice            # terminal 3: SPACE arms; type: hover

    On arming it makes a few short pushes to learn which way it faces, then holds position. Let it hover a minute before anyone says "follow me". Someone stands next to the battery switch the whole time.

9. Before the first flight, in one place

Go / no-go.
  • ble_gondola --probe shows IMU lines at 20+ per second, gz positive when turned counter-clockwise.
  • Each teleop key moves exactly the motor the plan view says, in the direction it says.
  • Ctrl+C on the teleop stops every motor within half a second; the firmware timeout is confirmed, or a person is on the switch.
  • Every motor on foam tape, props balanced, nothing within 3 cm of a prop disc.
  • Gondola weighed, PHYS updated, the scenario suite passes.
  • Balloon plain white, at the diameter written in PHYS, ring on the neck.
  • Gondola hangs level, every prop tip 5 cm or more from the skin, trimmed about one gram heavy.
  • Room: ceiling 2.5 m or more, no vents, the laptop webcam sees the mat and the whole balloon.
  • Battery switch reachable, and someone standing next to it.

10. Ask the hardware team

  • Command timeout in the firmware: no command for 500 ms means STOP. Today the laptop is the only failsafe; if that process dies the last percentages keep running.
  • Which motor drivers: an L298N drops about 2 V. On a 3.7 V cell the motors barely turn; either a 7.4 V battery with motors rated for it, or a DRV8833 / TB6612 / MX1508 board.
  • Percent to PWM: 30 should mean 30 % duty. A minimum or a curve in the firmware is fine if the software side knows (DUTY_MIN, T_MAX).
  • IMU line rate: 20+ notifications a second of A:…;G:…;T:…, gyro in deg/s. Bench 2026-09-19: one a second arrived, and the laptop prints every notification the moment it comes in, so the slow part is in the firmware or the radio. Add a counter ;N:123 (+1 per notify) to the line: N counting 1, 2, 3 at the laptop = the firmware notifies once a second; N jumping by ~100 = the radio drops them, so notify every 20-50 ms, not every 10.
  • One battery, one ground, one switch, and a bulk capacitor so a four-motor start does not reset the board.
  • Bluetooth drops were seen on the bench. The bridge reconnects and its last word is STOP; the firmware timeout is what makes a drop safe.
